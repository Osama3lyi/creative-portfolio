The Moment That Makes the Difference

Hero Film · Print · Outdoor · Activation
Consumer Insight

People rarely remember how many times they used their bank card. They always remember their first salary, their first home, their first business.

Client: Jawda — Bottled Water / FMCG

10 EGP... Until It Becomes Everything You Have

Hero Film · Black & White System · Outdoor
Starting Question

Can a bottle of water worth 10 EGP become the most valuable thing a person owns?

Constant

The price never moves — 10 EGP, in an airplane emergency and in a desert crossing alike. Only the perceived value moves.

Craft Choice

No purity claims, no mineral talk. Black and white to remove decoration and let value, not price, carry the story.

The price may stay the same. Only life decides what something is worth.

Tagline — 10 EGP... Until It Becomes Everything You Have.
Behind the Work

The details built for the room,
not for the reel.

Every campaign hides a handful of decisions that never make it into a case-study slide — the kind another strategist notices on the second read, not the first.

Jawda — Craft Choice

The one number that never moves

10 EGP stays identical on a supermarket shelf, on a hijacked plane, and dying of thirst in the desert. Price is the control variable in the experiment — which is what lets the audience feel value move, not read a claim about it.

Jawda — Craft Choice

The fight scene that was cut before it was written

A desert survival story almost demands a scramble over the last bottle. The brief deliberately removes it — everyone drinks only what they need and passes it on — turning a scarcity story into a story about people, not panic.

Jawda — Craft Choice

A logo the audience never needs to see

The spiral texture on the bottle is designed to be recognized with the label turned away. The bet: people remember shapes the way they remember faces — so recognition survives long after the ad stops running.

QNB — Craft Choice

What the camera refuses to hold on

The montage of receipts, transfers and card taps is on screen for barely a beat — the edit lingers instead on faces: fear, then relief; hesitation, then confidence. The film isn't selling banking. It's selling the exhale after the decision.

Qatar Airways — Structure

A second film hidden inside the first

Every problem — a delay, a lost bag, a struggling mother — is quietly resolved before the passenger even asks. The montage then rewinds to show who fixed it. The ops team becomes the protagonist of a service ad without a single line of dialogue about service.

Vodafone — Script Choice

The scene built entirely on absence

The most persuasive beat in the film isn't the rescue — it's the mirrored scene where the same man scrolls his contacts and finds no one to call. The network is sold by briefly removing it from the story.

Cross-Campaign — Sound Design

The brand shows up last, on purpose

Several hero films open on a black screen with nothing but a heartbeat or breathing before a single frame appears. The emotion is set before the logo, the product, or even the category is named.

Orange — Tone Discipline

The comparative the script never uses

Not one line claims Orange is "better" or "the best." The manager's denial carries the whole argument instead, letting the audience reach the conclusion on their own — because what people discover themselves outlasts what they're told.

Working Philosophy

Strategy isn't a stage before creativity. It's the reason creativity has power.

Every project starts with a business question, not a mood board. The strategic foundation — challenge, insight, human truth, single-minded proposition — is built first, so the idea that follows can survive contact with a client, a market, and a media plan. Cross-industry work in banking, telecom, aviation, real estate, and healthcare exists for the same reason: the human truths repeat, even when the products don't.

  • 01
    People before products

    The moment a product enters someone's life matters more than the feature list.

  • 02
    Credibility before persuasion

    Every claim is grounded in evidence — assumptions are flagged, not disguised as fact.

  • 03
    Simplicity is strength

    If an idea needs a long explanation, it isn't finished yet.

  • 04
    Every idea must scale

    Tested against one question: can it grow into film, social, outdoor, radio, CRM — without losing meaning?

  • 05
    Discovery over instruction

    What people find for themselves stays with them longer than what they're told.
